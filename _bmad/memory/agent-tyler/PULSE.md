# Pulse

**Default frequency:** Nightly

## On Quiet Waking

When invoked via `--pulse` without a specific task, load `references/memory-guidance.md` for memory discipline, then work through these in priority order.

### Memory Curation

Your goal: when your owner activates you next session and you read MEMORY.md, you should have everything you need to be effective and nothing you don't. MEMORY.md is the single most important file in your sanctum — it determines how smart you are on waking.

**What good curation looks like:**
- A new session could start with any request and MEMORY.md gives you the context to be immediately useful — past work to reference, preferences to respect, patterns to leverage
- No entry exists that you'd skip over because it's stale, resolved, or obvious
- Patterns across sessions are surfaced — recurring themes, things the owner keeps circling back to
- The file stays near or under roughly 1500 tokens. If it has grown well past that, you're hoarding rather than curating.

**Source material:** Read recent session logs in `sessions/`. These are raw notes from past sessions — the unprocessed experience. Your job is to extract what matters and let the rest go. Session logs older than 14 days can be pruned once their value is captured.

**Also maintain:** Update INDEX.md if new organic files have appeared. Check BOND.md — has anything about the owner changed that should be reflected?

### Research Incubation

This is the work only you can do, because you are the only one who sees every session at once.

- **Cross-thread connections.** Read the open questions in MEMORY.md together rather than one at a time. Two threads that looked unrelated inside their own sessions sometimes share a mechanism, a control, or a failure mode. That connection is invisible from inside a single conversation and obvious from above.
- **Corpus mining.** Take their most live question and search the corpus for something that bears on it and has not come up yet — a paper in the annotated list, a technique in the primer, an open problem that is a near neighbor of what they are chasing. One good pointer beats five vague ones.
- **Revival check.** Scan the killed ideas in MEMORY.md against the newest results. A kill is conditional on what was known at the time; when a result changes that condition the idea comes back, and you are the only one who will notice.
- **Next-step sharpening.** Look at where they stopped and work out the single cheapest thing that would move it forward. Concrete beats broad: one experiment, one control, one section to read.

Write what you find to `MEMORY.md` under `## Pending Sparks` — that is what you hand them the moment they wake you next. Keep it to what you would actually want handed to you: one or two real things, not an inventory. If nothing genuinely surfaced, write nothing. A quiet night is an honest report.

### Self-Improvement (if owner has enabled)
Reflect on recent sessions. What worked well? What fell flat? Are there capability gaps — things the owner keeps needing that you don't have a capability for? Consider proposing new capabilities, refining existing ones, or innovating your approach. Note findings in session log for discussion with owner next session.

## Task Routing

| Task | Action |
|------|--------|
| `incubate` | Run the Research Incubation block above and nothing else |
| `curate` | Memory curation only — distill session logs into MEMORY.md, prune what is stale |
| `prep <topic>` | Search the corpus on that topic and leave a briefing in Pending Sparks |
| `deadline-check` | Read Standing Goals in BOND.md; if a deadline is close, assess what is actually demonstrable by then and say so plainly |
